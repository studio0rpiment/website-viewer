Before you go

A city built around open space.

Washington was laid out with circles, squares and reservations from the start, and that legacy is still easy to feel. Many of the parks below are managed by the National Park Service, while others belong to the D.C. Department of Parks and Recreation.

You do not need a plan to enjoy them. Pick a neighborhood, take Metro or a bike, and stay a while. This guide groups the parks by area and by what you feel like doing, so you can find the right one in a minute.

Getting around

Metrorail and Metrobus reach most of these parks, and Capital Bikeshare docks are common near the busier ones. Trail-heavy parks like Rock Creek and Anacostia Park are especially pleasant by bike.

Pack light

Bring water, sun protection and a blanket. Some parks have few food options nearby, so a small picnic goes a long way.

Timing

Weekday mornings are the calmest. Weekend afternoons, especially in warm months, bring markets, music and crowds.

Check first

Hours, events and trail conditions change. Look up the National Park Service or D.C. Parks and Recreation page for a park before you head out.

Spring

Blossoms and fresh green along the Potomac and in the neighborhood parks.

Summer

Water lilies and lotus at Kenilworth, evening picnics and outdoor events.

Fall

Golden leaves along the Rock Creek trails and cooler, quieter walks.

Winter

Bare-branch views, few crowds and skating in the right conditions.
